# The Pythagorean theorem, seen — the script

## scene s01_setup: the square on the hypotenuse

[s01.1] Here is a right triangle. Its legs are three and four, and its hypotenuse is five — the most famous triple there is.
[s01.2] The theorem says the square on the hypotenuse equals the two squares on the legs, added together. {squares_draw}But why?

## scene s02_proof: the rearrangement

[s02.1] Take four copies of that triangle. {four_copies}They pack into a square of side seven — three plus four — in two different ways.
[s02.2] Packed one way, {pack_a}the four triangles leave two squares: side three, and side four.
[s02.3] Packed the other way, {pack_b}the same four triangles leave one square: side five — the hypotenuse.
[s02.4] Same square of side seven. Same four triangles. {both_shown}So what is left must be equal: nine plus sixteen equals twenty five.

## scene s03_land: the close

[s03.1] Three squared plus four squared equals five squared. {equation_lands}Nine plus sixteen, twenty five. Every right triangle, always.
